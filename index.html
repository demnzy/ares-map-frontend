The problem & science mission

Global Mars maps show the whole planet. Astronauts need a map for the next four hours on foot.

As Artemis II showed during its lunar flyby, human explorers observe and connect clues in the field in ways rovers cannot. Future astronauts walking across Jezero Crater's western delta (18.38°N, 77.58°E) to sample ancient river mudstones and crater-rim carbonates will need the same real-time awareness on the ground.

Existing NASA tools like JPL Mars Trek (which offers both 2D and 3D views) and the USGS-NASA Planetary Geologic Mapping Program are built for regional and global planning. They do not layer hourly weather forecasts, wind-blown dust, cosmic radiation, and 15° spacesuit slope limits onto the hyperlocalized 4.1 × 4.1 km walking zone an astronaut explores on a single Marswalk.

Extreme cold swings (−92°C to +19°C)

In Perseverance's weather logs, ground temperatures drop to −92°C before dawn and warm above freezing at midday. Walking into shadowed crater walls drains suit heaters fast.

Dust that cuts visibility (τ = 0.18 to 2.51)

Wind lifts fine dust into low basins and cuts how far crew can see and how much power solar panels generate.

Unshielded radiation (9.7 to 13.0 µGy/hr)

Mars has no global magnetic field and a thin atmosphere (623 to 808 Pa). Open ridges take the full cosmic ray dose, while crater walls offer partial cover.

How it works

From NASA rover logs to a 3D surface map in four steps.

1

Pick a day and time

Choose a Martian day (Sol) and hour of the day at Jezero Crater.

2

Predict the weather

A PyTorch neural network trained on 2,511 NASA weather and radiation rows predicts temperature, dust thickness, radiation, and wind in under 3 milliseconds.

3

Pack four hazards into one image

Our FastAPI server combines the forecast with HiRISE elevation slopes and shadows, packing all four hazard scores into the Red, Green, Blue, and Alpha channels of one PNG.

4

Load into the 3D simulator

The 3D terrain engine drapes that RGBA image over a 4096×4096 heightmap of Jezero Crater to color-code danger zones and plan safe walking routes.

Hazard layers

Four hazards packed into one RGBA image.

Instead of sending four separate files to the 3D engine, the backend packs all four hazard layers into one PNG image (dynamic_hazard_mask.png). Every pixel stores a score from 0 (safe) to 1 (high risk) in its red, green, blue, and alpha channels.

RRed channel0 warm, 1 freezing

Thermal

Highlights dangerously cold spots, such as shadowed crater walls and low basins where cold air settles overnight.

Inputs: ground temperature forecast, sun angle shadows, elevation, and night cooling.

GGreen channel0 clear, 1 heavy dust

Dust

Shows where blowing dust builds up and cuts visibility along the predicted wind direction.

Inputs: dust optical depth (τ), wind speed, wind bearing, and low-ground settling.

BBlue channel0 sheltered, 1 exposed

Radiation

Maps cosmic ray exposure, which stays higher on open ridges and drops inside steep crater walls.

Inputs: radiation dose forecast, ground elevation, and open-sky view factor.

AAlpha channel0 flat, 1 blocked (≥15°)

Slope

Flags ground steeper than 15 degrees, where a bulky pressurized suit can tip over.

Inputs: HiRISE elevation gradients (1 m/px) normalized to the 15° tipping limit.

Built for the Marswalk & 3D Engine

Designed for astronauts on foot, spacesuit limits, and live 3D rendering.

ARES-MAP works as a pre-EVA habitat briefing display, a carried crew field tablet, or a compact visor HUD overlay. Because pressurized suits restrict bending and can tip on steep rubble, the 3D scene layers live weather onto high-resolution terrain meshes so A* pathfinding routes two-person buddy teams between science stations while avoiding cliffs 15° or steeper and hazard pockets.

4096 px

16-bit heightmaps & 9×9 tiles

Converts HiRISE DTEEC_045994_1985 elevation (.IMG) and ESP_046060_1985 satellite imagery (.JP2) into 16-bit heightmaps plus 81 lossless ortho tiles so interactive habitat briefings and field tablets stream sharp terrain cleanly.

.GLB / .OBJ

3D delta & crater meshes

Exports binary glTF (.glb) and Wavefront (.obj) meshes with vertex normals for the 4.14 km NASA western delta (164 m relief) alongside our 45 km procedural full Jezero Crater model (320 m relief).

WebGL 2

Real-time shader & route planning

Drives our interactive Three.js 3D route explorer and Godot 4 Compatibility (COOP/COEP) client shaders (ares_terrain.gdshader), unpacking the RGBA mask in real time to flag 15° suit limits, cold basins, radiation ridges, and dust haze.
